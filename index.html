The problem

We live in a world that confuses new with better.

Today, the world lives in a cycle of constant consumption. We are encouraged to buy new devices every year, even when the ones we have still work perfectly. Millions of working phones, computers and gadgets are thrown away just because a newer model has appeared. This culture of "buy, use, discard" is not just expensive. It is unsustainable.

Every new device carries a cost that is hidden, but real.

Mining

Minerals found in only a few places in the world are extracted, destroying ecosystems and poisoning waterways.

Manufacturing

Factories burn energy to produce new units, creating pollution and far too much carbon.

Transport

Moving parts and products across the world adds to our footprint.

Disposal

Functional devices end up in landfills as toxic waste that may never decompose.

Better to keep and improve than to throw away and replace.

Our story

Progress does notrequire waste.

Our founders started Ketsu-sui in Tokyo. They were working as engineers for a big electronics brand, and every year they watched perfectly good devices being thrown away. One day, they decided to do something different: design devices that last for years, not just seasons.

We don't want you to buy less. We want you to value more.

Our solution

Long-Life Technology.

Instead of sealed, disposable products, Ketsu-sui devices are modular and repairable. When one part gets old or breaks, you change that part, not the whole device. This respects natural resources, reduces waste and gives people better value for their money.

Japanese values

Inspired by old ideas.
Built for the future.

Ketsu-sui is not just about technology. It is built on centuries of Japanese wisdom: value what you have, improve what exists, and respect the resources entrusted to us.

もったいない

Mottainai

Waste nothing

A feeling of regret when something useful is wasted, and deep respect for things that still have value.

How it guides usWe design products that are repaired and reused. Nothing is discarded unnecessarily.

改善

Kaizen

Always better

Continuous improvement: making things better without starting over.

How it guides usWe offer upgrades and improvements. You don't need to replace everything to have the best.

ものづくり

Monozukuri

Craft

The art of making things with excellence and pride: quality over quantity.

How it guides usWe build with premium materials so our products last for generations.

和

Wa

Harmony

Harmony and balance between people, nature and our possessions.

How it guides usOur technology improves life without harming the shared environment.

Kintsugi · 金継ぎ

Broken, then more beautiful.

In Japan, we do not discard a bowl just because it has a small chip. We may even repair it with gold, making it more beautiful because it has been cared for. We see repair the same way: not as a failure, but as something valuable.

Hari-Kuyō · 針供養

A thank-you for broken needles.

Even today, during the Hari-Kuyō festival, people take their old, broken sewing needles to temples and shrines to thank them for their service. That is what we offer the world: to care for our technology rather than abandon it.

You do not need to tear down a house to improve a room.

Questions & answers

Ask Ketsu-sui.

01Why is your proposal ethically responsible?

Because it respects our planet's limited resources and offers people an affordable alternative: you don't need to spend more money to have good technology. It creates a fairer relationship between people and the products we use.

02How does Japanese culture influence your project?

Our entire philosophy comes from Japanese values: Mottainai, respect for what we have; Monozukuri, pride in quality; and Kaizen, continuous improvement without waste. Our culture doesn't just inspire the project, it is the project.

03What makes your company different from competitors?

Most companies profit when you buy new products. We profit when you get the most life from the products you already own. Our success is measured by how long our devices last, not by how many we sell.

04What challenges would your business face internationally?

It might be difficult to convince consumers who are used to buying a new phone every year, and we will have to compete with big brands that profit from fast replacement. That is why education and marketing are as important for us as engineering.

05How realistic is your proposal economically?

Extending product life reduces costs for consumers and businesses. Repair and refurbishment create local jobs. Less waste means lower environmental cleanup costs. In the long term, durability is not just ethical, it is smarter economics.

06How would your company impact local communities?

In Colombia, new technical jobs will be created in our repair centers, young technicians will be trained, and less electronic waste will end up in local landfills.

07What ethical risks could your proposal create?

Refurbished devices must be safe, and old devices may contain personal data. That is why every refurbished unit is checked carefully, and all personal data is deleted before the device is reused.
